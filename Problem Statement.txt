Problem Statement No-4 – Making Learning Intelligent using AI
Engineering colleges typically deliver the same curriculum, learning resources and assessment
schedule to an entire class, even though students differ significantly in:
• Prior knowledge
• Learning speed
• Conceptual understanding
• Study behaviour

• Preferred learning resources
• Assessment performance
• Areas of difficulty
• Engagement with learning material
The challenge is to build an AI-powered Learning Intelligence Platform that continuously
analyses student learning data and recommends what a student should learn next, how they
should learn it, and when faculty intervention may be required.
Dataset Starter Pack
EdNet — Recommended flagship dataset -EdNet is one of the strongest datasets for this
hackathon. It contains more than 131 million student interactions from 784,309 students
collected over more than two years. It includes question-solving and other learning behaviours.
MathE — Higher Education Mathematics- The UCI repository includes the MathE dataset with
9,546 answers to mathematics questions in higher education.
UCI Higher Education Students Performance -The UCI Higher Education Students
Performance Evaluation dataset contains data from engineering and educational-science
students and was specifically collected to predict end-of-term performance. It has 145
instances and 31 features
UCI Student Academics Performance- Another useful benchmark is the UCI Student
Academics Performance dataset, available under CC BY 4.0
Indian Government Education Data- For an India-specific dimension, teams can use the
Government of India's Open Government Data platform.
Expected Outcomes:
“What does the student actually understand, what are they likely to struggle with next, and what
learning intervention will help?”
